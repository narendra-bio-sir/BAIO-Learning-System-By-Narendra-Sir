Biuret Test for Proteins

A simple colour-change test tells you, in under two minutes, whether an unknown solution contains protein. Press play to add sodium hydroxide and dilute copper sulphate to the sample, step by step, and watch for the violet colour that only peptide bonds can produce.

Aim

To test a given unknown sample solution for the presence of proteins using the Biuret test.

Principle

Proteins are chains of amino acids joined by peptide bonds (–CO–NH–). In a strongly alkaline medium, dilute copper sulphate provides Cu²⁺ ions that coordinate with the nitrogen and oxygen atoms of at least two neighbouring peptide bonds, forming a violet-to-purple coloured copper–peptide complex.

The test is named after biuret (H₂N–CO–NH–CO–NH₂), a small compound with two amide groups that gives the very same violet colour under identical conditions — which is how the reaction was first recognised. Any molecule with two or more peptide bonds, not biuret itself, will test positive; single amino acids and short dipeptides do not.

Method

Requirements

You need: unknown sample solution · 40% NaOH solution · 1% copper sulphate (CuSO₂) solution · clean test tubes · dropper · test tube stand

Procedure

  1. Take 2 mL of the unknown sample solution in a clean, dry test tube.
  2. Add 1 mL of 40% NaOH solution and mix well to make the medium strongly alkaline.
  3. Add 2–3 drops of 1% copper sulphate solution slowly, along the inner side of the tube.
  4. Shake the tube gently to mix the contents.
  5. Note the colour that develops within a minute.

Inference
  • A violet or purple colour in the unknown sample confirms the presence of protein — specifically, of two or more peptide bonds.
  • No colour change (the solution stays the pale blue of the added CuSO₂) means the sample does not contain protein.
  • The intensity of the violet colour roughly indicates how much protein is present — a deeper violet means more peptide bonds are reacting with Cu²⁺.
Precautions
  • Add the copper sulphate solution drop by drop — excess CuSO₂ turns the mixture blue again and can mask the violet colour.
  • Use clean, grease-free test tubes to avoid a false result.
  • Do not heat the tube — the Biuret test works at room temperature; heating is used in the Benedict's test, not this one.
