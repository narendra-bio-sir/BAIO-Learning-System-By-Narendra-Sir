📊 Which chapters do NEET & KCET really ask?

We read 1,050 Biology questions from 15 past papers (NEET, AIPMT, KCET and COMEDK) and sorted each one into its NCERT chapter. Here is what that means for how you study.

      3Which exam decides what?

      NEET fills MBBS and BDS seats in Karnataka, including government seats through KEA counselling. If you want a medical seat, the NEET bars are the ones to follow.

      KCET Biology counts for agriculture, veterinary, pharmacy and allied courses. It leans heavily on II PUC, so a strong II PUC base pays off here.

      COMEDK used to have Biology for medical seats and is now an engineering test. Its old Biology questions are still good practice, because they test the same NCERT facts.

      4How many marks do you need?

      🎯 The numbers to aim for

      In 2025 the NEET qualifying mark for the general category was reported as 144 out of 720. Qualifying is not the same as getting a seat.

      Government MBBS seats in Karnataka closed much higher. News reports put Bangalore Medical College near 650 and KIMS near 606 out of 720 for the 2025 general category. Check the official KEA lists at kea.kar.nic.in for your category.

      For a government seat, aim for about 85–90% in Biology. That is roughly 77–81 right answers out of 90, with very few wrong ones, because every wrong answer costs a mark.

      5How your BAIO practice lines up

      We checked 80 past questions by hand against the BAIO app's practice questions. In chapters that have a full BAIO chapter page, 45 of 61 past questions tested the same fact as a BAIO practice question, and 56 of 61 had at least a closely related one. So if you finish a BAIO chapter properly, you have already practised most of what the past papers asked from it.

      Ten I PUC chapters do not have a full BAIO page yet. Together they carried about 1 in 5 past NEET Biology questions, and none of the 19 we checked from them matched a BAIO practice question. For these chapters, read the NCERT textbook line by line and do their PYQs here:

      6A study plan that follows the data

      1. Pick your next chapter from the barsSort the carousel by your exam. Start with the tallest bars you have not finished yet.
      2. Learn it from the chapter pageRead the BAIO chapter page, then that chapter's NEET Tricks for the traps examiners like.
      3. Drill itDo the chapter MCQs, flash cards and Diagram MCQs until you get most of them right.
      4. Test yourself on its PYQsOpen the chapter in NEET & KCET PYQs. Aim for 8 out of 10 right before you move on.
      5. Do one full past paper every weekIn PYQs, switch to “By paper” and do one paper in one sitting, without looking at the answers.
      6. Fix every mistakeRead the explanation and find the NCERT line it came from. Mark it, because that line can come again in a new question.

      7Read this before you trust the bars

      • Past papers show what examiners have liked. They do not promise what comes next. Any NCERT line can be asked, so don't skip a chapter because its bar is short.
      • These are 15 papers, most of them from 2013–2021. The pattern of newer NEET papers can be different.
      • Questions on topics removed from the current NCERT syllabus are not counted, so every bar is about what you study today.
      • No app or past paper can guarantee a score or a seat. Regular NCERT reading and honest practice do the work. The app helps you practise the right things.

      How this was counted. Every Biology question in the 15 papers was tagged to one NCERT chapter by hand. 171 questions were left out: topics dropped from the rationalised NCERT (for example reproduction in organisms, mineral nutrition, transport in plants, digestion, animal husbandry and ecological succession), KCET questions that KEA gave grace marks for, and a few unreadable lines in a scanned paper. That leaves 879 questions: NEET 225, AIPMT 145, KCET 409 and COMEDK 100. 726 of them are in the app as free PYQ practice (KCET 2015–2021 and NEET/AIPMT 2013–2016). The “3 in 4” and “45 of 61” figures come from 80 randomly chosen past questions that were checked by hand against the app's practice questions.
