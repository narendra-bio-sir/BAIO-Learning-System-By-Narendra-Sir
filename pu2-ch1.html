Board Important – Karnataka 2nd PUC Question Bank

Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants is one of the most diagram-heavy, structure-based chapters in 2nd PUC Biology – almost every topic yields a labelled-diagram question, and it is equally important for NEET/KCET.
~14–16 marksConsistently one of the highest-weightage chapters, heavy on labelled diagrams
5 × 5 MAnther & microsporogenesis, embryo sac development, double fertilisation, pollination types/agents, seed & fruit structure
5 × 3 MPollen grain structure, outbreeding devices, self-incompatibility, dicot/monocot embryo, apomixis & polyembryony
5 × 2 MPlacentation types, pollen-pistil interaction terms, emasculation & bagging, endosperm types, parthenocarpy

⭐⭐⭐ Top priority – almost always asked (5 marks)

  • T.S. of anther & microsporogenesis (5 M) – draw a dithecous, tetrasporangiate anther in T.S.: label epidermis, endothecium, 2–3 middle layers, and the innermost nutritive tapetum surrounding each microsporangium; describe meiosis of microspore mother cells forming microspore tetrads.
  • Development of the female gametophyte (embryo sac) (5 M) – megaspore mother cell (2n) undergoes meiosis → 4 megaspores (n), 3 degenerate (monosporic development) → functional megaspore undergoes 3 free-nuclear mitotic divisions → 8-nucleate, 7-celled embryo sac: 3 antipodals, 2 polar nuclei in central cell, egg apparatus (1 egg + 2 synergids with filiform apparatus).
  • Double fertilisation (5 M) – pollen tube enters through a synergid (porogamy) and releases 2 male gametes; one fuses with the egg (syngamy) → diploid zygote; the other fuses with the 2 polar nuclei (triple fusion) → triploid (3n) primary endosperm nucleus. Coined by S.G. Nawaschin.
  • Pollination – types and agents (5 M) – autogamy, geitonogamy, xenogamy; abiotic (anemophily, hydrophily) vs biotic (entomophily, ornithophily, chiropterophily) agents with floral adaptations for each (e.g. large, sticky pollen and nectar for insect pollination; light, non-sticky pollen for wind pollination).
  • Structure of an anatropous ovule, seed & fruit development (5 M) – ovule parts (funicle, hilum, micropyle, chalaza, integuments, nucellus, embryo sac); after fertilisation ovule → seed, ovary → fruit, ovary wall → pericarp (epicarp, mesocarp, endocarp).

⭐⭐ High priority – frequently asked (3 marks)

  • Structure of a pollen grain (3 M) – two-layered wall: hard, sporopollenin-covered exine with germ pores, and inner intine of cellulose/pectin; cytoplasm has a vegetative cell and a smaller generative cell.
  • Outbreeding devices in flowers (3 M) – dichogamy (protandry/protogyny), herkogamy (physical separation of anther & stigma), unisexuality (dioecy) – all prevent autogamy and promote cross-pollination.
  • Self-incompatibility (3 M) – a genetic mechanism preventing self-pollen (or pollen from a genetically similar plant) from fertilising the same or a related flower, by inhibiting pollen germination or pollen-tube growth in the pistil.
  • Structure of a dicot embryo (3 M) – radicle, embryonal axis, plumule, and two cotyledons; label the epicotyl (above cotyledon attachment) and hypocotyl (below it) regions.
  • Apomixis & polyembryony (3 M) – apomixis is asexual reproduction that produces seeds without fertilisation (e.g. some Asteraceae/grasses); polyembryony is the occurrence of more than one embryo in a seed, common in Citrus and Mango (nucellar polyembryony).

⭐ Two-mark favourites – learn as clean pairs

  • Marginal placentation (single ovary chamber, e.g. pea) vs axile placentation (multi-chambered, e.g. china rose, tomato).
  • Autogamy (self within same flower) vs geitonogamy (genetically self, but pollinator-mediated between flowers of the same plant) vs xenogamy (genetically cross, between two different plants).
  • Emasculation (removal of anthers from a bisexual flower before dehiscence) vs bagging (covering the emasculated flower to prevent contamination) in artificial hybridisation.
  • Nuclear endosperm (free nuclear divisions first, e.g. coconut water) vs cellular endosperm (wall formation right after each division).
  • Parthenocarpy (fruit development without fertilisation, seedless, e.g. banana) vs apomixis (seed formation without fertilisation).
Answer-writing tip: for every structure-based 5-mark question (anther, ovule, embryo sac), draw a clean, large, correctly-proportioned diagram FIRST and then write labels pointing to it – Karnataka board examiners award separate marks for the diagram and for the labels/description.
Never lose these marks: always specify the exact ploidy at each stage – megaspore mother cell (2n), megaspore (n), embryo sac cells (n), zygote (2n), primary endosperm nucleus (3n) – and never confuse syngamy (egg + 1 male gamete) with triple fusion (2 polar nuclei + 1 male gamete).

Important Diagrams

📖 Every high-yield figure in this chapter, ranked by board-exam priority with its typical mark weightage and the exact labels you must show — so you always know which diagrams matter most.

Male & Female Reproductive Structures

Fig 1.1 — T.S. of Anther & Microsporangium 5 M ★★★ Top Priority
Draw a dithecous, tetrasporangiate anther. Label the 4 wall layers of each microsporangium from outside in: epidermis, endothecium (fibrous thickenings, helps dehiscence), 2–3 middle layers, tapetum (innermost, nourishes developing pollen). Show the sporogenous tissue/microspore mother cells in the centre.
Fig 1.2 — Mature 8-Nucleate, 7-Celled Embryo Sac 5 M ★★★ Top Priority
Label the micropylar end (egg apparatus: 1 egg cell + 2 synergids with filiform apparatus), the central cell (2 polar nuclei), and the chalazal end (3 antipodal cells). Show it enclosed within the nucellus and integuments of the ovule.

Pollen, Ovule & Fertilisation

Fig 1.3 — Structure of a Pollen Grain 3 M ★★☆ High Priority
Label the outer exine (hard, sporopollenin, with germ pores), inner intine (cellulose & pectin), and inside the cytoplasm: the larger vegetative cell and smaller generative cell (which later divides into 2 male gametes).
Fig 1.4 — Anatropous Ovule & Pollen Tube Growth 5 M ★★★ Top Priority
Label funicle, hilum, micropyle, chalaza, two integuments, and nucellus enclosing the embryo sac. Show the pollen tube growing through the style and entering via the micropyle (porogamy), carrying the 2 male gametes to a synergid.
Fig 1.5 — Double Fertilisation Schematic 5 M ★★★ Top Priority
Show one male gamete fusing with the egg cell (syngamy → 2n zygote) and the other fusing with the two polar nuclei in the central cell (triple fusion → 3n primary endosperm nucleus). Clearly label both events as together constituting "double fertilisation".

Seed, Fruit & Embryo Structure

Fig 1.6 — Structure of a Dicot Embryo 3 M ★★☆ High Priority
Label the embryonal axis, plumule (shoot tip) at the epicotyl end, radicle (root tip) at the hypocotyl end, and the two cotyledons attached laterally.
Fig 1.7 — L.S. of a Monocot (Grass) Seed 2 – 3 M ★☆☆ Occasional
Label the single large cotyledon (scutellum), coleoptile (covers plumule), coleorhiza (covers radicle), and the endosperm as the main food-storage tissue (unlike dicots, food is not stored in the cotyledon).
