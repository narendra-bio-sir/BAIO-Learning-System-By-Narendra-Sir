1. Genetics Basics & Mendel's Experiments

  • Genetics = branch of biology studying inheritance & variation. Gene = Mendel's "factor"; allele = alternate form of a gene at a locus.
  • Homozygous (TT/tt) vs Heterozygous (Tt); Monohybrid (1 character) vs Dihybrid (2 characters).
  • Test cross = hybrid × homozygous recessive; Out cross = hybrid × homozygous dominant.
  • Mendel (father of genetics) – garden pea (Pisum sativum), 1856–63, 7 pairs of contrasting characters (stem height, seed shape, seed colour, pod shape, pod colour, flower colour, flower position).

2. Monohybrid Cross & Mendel's Laws

  • TT × tt → F1 all Tt (tall) → selfing → F2 3:1 phenotypic (Tall:Dwarf), 1:2:1 genotypic (TT:Tt:tt).
  • Law of Dominance: one factor of a dissimilar pair dominates the other. Law of Segregation: alleles separate cleanly during gamete formation (Law of Purity of Gametes).
  • Punnett square – Reginald C. Punnett; F2 ratio derived from (ax+by)².
  • Test cross case 1: TT×tt → all Tt (homozygous dominant). Case 2: Tt×tt → 1:1 Tt:tt (heterozygous).
  • Guinea pig coat colour: BB(black)×bb(white) → F1 all Bb black → F2 3:1 black:white.

3. Dihybrid Cross & Independent Assortment

  • RRYY × rryy → F1 RrYy (round, yellow) → selfing → F2 9:3:3:1 (9 Round Yellow : 3 Round Green : 3 Wrinkled Yellow : 1 Wrinkled Green).
  • F2 genotypic ratio 1:2:1:2:4:2:1:2:1; 9:3:3:1 = (3 Round:1 Wrinkled)×(3 Yellow:1 Green).
  • Law of Independent Assortment: two pairs of traits segregate independently of each other.
  • Mendel's work published 1865, rediscovered 1900 by Hugo de Vries, Correns, Von Tschermak.

4. Deviations from Mendelism

  • Incomplete dominance (Carl Correns, 1903): F1 is an intermediate mixture; RR×rr → Rr pink; F2 1:2:1 (Red:Pink:White) – phenotypic ratio = genotypic ratio. Andalusian fowl: Black×White → Blue F1.
  • Co-dominance: both alleles express fully & independently; AB blood group, sickle cell carriers (HbAHbS), roan cattle (RW).
  • Multiple alleles: >2 allele forms in a population; ABO blood group – gene I with alleles IA, IB, i (Landsteiner 1901; Bernstein 1924) – 6 genotypes, 4 phenotypes.
  • Pleiotropy: one gene → many traits; starch synthesis in pea (BB/Bb/bb), PKU (Folling 1934, chromosome 12), sickle cell anaemia.
  • Polygenic inheritance: trait controlled by 3+ genes, additive effect; human skin colour; F. Galton coined "eugenics"; Nilsson-Ehle – wheat kernel colour proof.

5. Chromosomal Theory of Inheritance & Linkage

  • Sutton & Boveri (1902): chromosome behaviour parallels gene behaviour → Chromosomal Theory of Inheritance.
  • T.H. Morgan (father of experimental genetics/sex linkage) – Drosophila melanogaster; F2 ratio deviated from 9:3:3:1 when genes were on the same (X) chromosome.
  • Linkage = genes physically associated on a chromosome; Recombination = non-parental combinations. y-w genes: 98.7% parental (tight linkage); w-m genes: 62.8% parental, 37.2% recombinant (loose linkage).
  • Sturtevant: recombination frequency → genetic maps. 1% crossing over = 1 map unit = 1 cM; 100% = 1 Morgan.

6. Sex Determination

  • Henking – discovered "X body" (later, X chromosome). Allosomes = sex chromosomes; Autosomes = the rest.
  • XO type (male heterogamety): female XX, male XO. Example – Grasshopper, Earthworm.
  • XY type (male heterogamety): female XX, male XY. Example – Humans, Drosophila.
  • ZW type (female heterogamety): female ZW, male ZZ. Example – Birds, some reptiles/fishes.
  • Haplodiploidy (honey bee): fertilised egg (diploid, 32 chr) → female; unfertilised egg (haploid, 16 chr, parthenogenesis) → male. Males make sperm by mitosis; no father, but can have grandsons.

7. Mutation & Pedigree Analysis

  • Mutation = alteration of DNA sequence. Mutagens: physical (UV, gamma), chemical (nitrous acid), biological (viruses/bacteria).
  • Point mutation = single base pair change (sickle cell anaemia). Frameshift mutation = insertion/deletion of a base pair.
  • Pedigree analysis – traces a trait across a family tree. Symbols: □ male, ◯ female, ◆ sex unspecified, filled = affected, double line = consanguineous mating, arrow = propositus, diagonal line = deceased.

8. Genetic Disorders

  • Autosomal dominant: mutant allele dominant; every generation affected. E.g. Huntington disease (ch4), Myotonic dystrophy.
  • Autosomal recessive: carrier parents, consanguinity risk. E.g. Sickle cell anaemia (ch11: GAG→GUG, Glu→Val at position 6 of beta chain), Thalassemia (alpha: ch16/HBA1&HBA2; beta: ch11/HBB), PKU (ch12).
  • X-linked dominant: Duchenne muscular dystrophy – no male-to-male transmission.
  • X-linked recessive: Haemophilia A (Factor VIII; "royal disease"; criss-cross inheritance), Colour blindness (~8% males, ~0.4% females).
  • Y-linked (holandric): Hypertrichosis – father → all sons, never daughters.
  • Chromosomal disorders (46 = 23 pairs = 22 autosomes + 1 allosome pair): Down's syndrome (trisomy 21), Klinefelter's (47, XXY), Turner's (45, XO), Cri du chat (deletion, short arm of chromosome 5).
