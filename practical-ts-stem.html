T.S. of Monocot Stem and Dicot Stem

A thin, even free-hand transverse section, stained with safranin, is the fastest way to see how a monocot and a dicot arrange their tissues completely differently — one in a neat ring around a defined pith, the other scattered right through the ground tissue. Below is the full procedure, followed by labelled diagrams of what you should actually see under the microscope.

Aim

To prepare temporary stained mounts of transverse sections (T.S.) of a dicot stem and a monocot stem, and to identify, label and compare their internal structure.

Principle

A section thin enough to let light pass through reveals the internal tissues under a microscope. Safranin selectively stains lignified and cutinised cell walls — xylem, sclerenchyma and the cuticle — pink to red, so the vascular and mechanical tissues stand out clearly against the pale, thin-walled parenchyma, letting you map out the tissue zones by colour and position.

Method

Materials Required

You need: a fresh dicot stem internode (e.g. sunflower/Helianthus or Tridax) · a fresh monocot stem internode (e.g. maize/Zea mays or grass) · a sharp double-edged razor blade · a piece of pith, carrot or thermocol to hold the specimen while cutting · watch glasses · camel-hair brush · safranin stain · glycerine · distilled water · glass slides & coverslips · blotting paper · compound microscope

Procedure

  1. Select the specimen — choose a fresh, straight, moderately thick internode (not a node) of the dicot stem.
  2. Cut thin sections — split a piece of pith or carrot, hold the stem upright in the split, and with a sharp razor blade wetted with water, draw the blade towards you in one smooth stroke (never saw back and forth) to cut very thin transverse slices.
  3. Transfer to water — lift each section immediately into a watch glass of water using a wet camel-hair brush.
  4. Select the thinnest — from the sections collected, pick out only the thinnest and most even ones; thick or uneven sections look dark and opaque and cannot be studied. Discard the rest.
  5. Stain — transfer the selected sections into a watch glass of safranin stain for 2–5 minutes.
  6. Wash — rinse off the excess stain gently with distilled water until the washings run almost colourless.
  7. Mount — place a stained section at the centre of a clean slide, add a drop of glycerine, and lower a coverslip from one edge with a needle to avoid trapping air bubbles.
  8. Observe — focus first under low power to locate the section, then switch to high power to study the arrangement of tissues.
  9. Repeat — repeat steps 1–8 with a fresh internode of the monocot stem to prepare its section for comparison.
Diagrams & Observation

Dicot Stem — T.S. (whole)

e.g. Sunflower — a single ring of vascular bundles around a large pith

  1. Epidermis — single outer layer, cuticle-covered
  2. Hypodermis — collenchymatous
  3. Cortex — parenchymatous ground tissue
  4. Endodermis — starch sheath, innermost cortical layer
  5. Pericycle — sclerenchymatous patches
  6. Vascular bundle — conjoint, collateral, open — in a ring
  7. Medullary ray — connects cortex to pith between bundles
  8. Pith — large, central, parenchymatous

Monocot Stem — T.S. (whole)

e.g. Maize — numerous bundles scattered through the ground tissue

  1. Epidermis — thick cuticle
  2. Hypodermis — sclerenchymatous (mechanical support)
  3. Ground tissue — undifferentiated — no separate cortex/pith
  4. Vascular bundles — scattered; smaller & crowded near the periphery, larger & sparser toward the centre

Closed bundle — no cambium, oval sclerenchymatous sheath

Key structural differences

Dicot stem — the ground tissue is clearly zoned into a narrow cortex (outside) and a large central pith (inside), separated by a single ring of conjoint, collateral and open vascular bundles — "open" because a strip of cambium lies between the phloem and xylem in every bundle, which is exactly what lets the stem add secondary growth later.

Monocot stem — there is no such zoning: the ground tissue is uniform from just under the hypodermis right to the centre. The vascular bundles are conjoint, collateral and closed (no cambium) and lie scattered through it — smaller and more crowded near the periphery, larger and more widely spaced toward the centre. Each bundle is capped by a sclerenchymatous bundle sheath and shows a conspicuous protoxylem lacuna.

Precautions
  • Sections must be as thin as possible — thick sections look dark and opaque and hide internal detail.
  • Always draw the blade towards yourself in one clean stroke; never saw back and forth.
  • Handle the razor blade with care at all times.
  • Do not over-stain — excess safranin obscures the distinction between tissues, so wash thoroughly.
  • Avoid trapping air bubbles under the coverslip while mounting.
  • Keep sections moist throughout — a dried-out section shrinks and distorts.
