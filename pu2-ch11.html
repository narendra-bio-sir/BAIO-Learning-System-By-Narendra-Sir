Board Important – Karnataka 2nd PUC Question Bank

Organisms and Populations mixes adaptation-based theory with graph/curve questions – population growth curves and interaction types are asked almost every year, and it's a favourite for NEET/KCET too.
~9–11 marksEcology unit chapter, strong on graphs, adaptations and interaction-pair definitions
4 × 5 MPopulation growth curves (J vs S-shaped), organismic responses to stress (adaptations), population interactions (types with examples), factors affecting population growth
5 × 3 MPopulation attributes, Gause's competitive exclusion principle, Bergmann's/Allen's rule, thermoregulation vs conformity, life-history variation (r & K selection)
5 × 2 MHibernation vs aestivation, mutualism examples, natality/mortality, age pyramid shapes, predation's ecological role

⭐⭐⭐ Top priority – almost always asked (5 marks)

  • Population growth curves – J-shaped vs S-shaped (5 M) – J-shaped (exponential): dN/dt = rN, occurs when resources are unlimited, curve rises indefinitely; S-shaped (logistic): dN/dt = rN(K−N)/K, growth slows as population nears carrying capacity (K) due to limited resources, more realistic in nature.
  • Organismic responses to environmental stress (5 M) – regulate (maintain homeostasis, e.g. birds/mammals thermoregulate), conform (body temperature/osmotic concentration changes with environment, e.g. most invertebrates/reptiles), migrate (temporarily move to a more favourable habitat, e.g. Siberian birds visiting Keoladeo National Park), suspend (dormancy: hibernation in winter, aestivation in summer, diapause in insects, spores in bacteria).
  • Types of population interactions with examples (5 M) – mutualism (+,+; e.g. lichens, mycorrhizae), competition (−,−; e.g. flamingo & fish competing for zooplankton), predation (+,−; e.g. lion & deer), parasitism (+,−; e.g. cuckoo brood parasitism, malarial parasite), commensalism (+,0; e.g. orchid on mango branch, cattle egret & grazing cattle), amensalism (−,0; e.g. Penicillium inhibiting bacterial growth).
  • Factors regulating population growth/density (5 M) – natality (births), mortality (deaths), immigration (individuals entering), emigration (individuals leaving); population density at time (t+1) = Nt + [(B + I) − (D + E)], where increase due to natality/immigration adds, decrease due to mortality/emigration subtracts.

⭐⭐ High priority – frequently asked (3 marks)

  • Population attributes (3 M) – unlike an individual organism, a population has attributes like birth rate/death rate (expressed as per capita), sex ratio, age distribution (age pyramid), and population density (number of individuals per unit area/volume).
  • Gause's competitive exclusion principle (3 M) – two closely related species competing for the same limiting resource cannot coexist indefinitely in the same habitat; the competitively superior species will eventually eliminate the other.
  • Bergmann's rule & Allen's rule (3 M) – Bergmann's rule: mammals in colder climates tend to have larger bodies (lower surface-area-to-volume ratio, minimises heat loss); related adaptations include shorter ears/limbs (Allen's rule) in colder regions to reduce heat loss.
  • Thermoregulation vs conformity (3 M) – regulators (birds, mammals) maintain constant internal temperature/osmotic concentration via physiological (sometimes behavioural) means despite external fluctuation, at high metabolic cost; conformers change their body temperature/osmotic concentration with the ambient environment, saving energy.
  • r-selected vs K-selected life-history strategies (3 M) – r-strategists (e.g. many insects, small fish) reproduce early with many small offspring in unstable/unpredictable environments; K-strategists (e.g. elephants, whales) reproduce later with fewer, larger, better-cared-for offspring near carrying capacity.

⭐ Two-mark favourites – learn as clean pairs

  • Hibernation (winter sleep, e.g. bear) vs aestivation (summer dormancy, e.g. snails, fish to avoid heat/desiccation).
  • Mutualism (both benefit, e.g. lichens = fungus + alga) vs commensalism (one benefits, other unaffected, e.g. orchid growing on a mango branch).
  • Natality (per capita births) vs mortality (per capita deaths) – both affect population density over time.
  • Expanding age pyramid (triangular, more young individuals, growing population) vs stable (bell-shaped) vs declining (urn-shaped, more aged individuals).
  • Predation's ecological role – keeps prey populations in check, can help maintain species diversity by reducing dominance of a single competitor.
Answer-writing tip: for any interaction/response type question, always give the (+/−/0) notation alongside a real named example – e.g. "predation (+,−) e.g. tiger & deer" – Karnataka board examiners specifically reward paired notation-plus-example answers.
Never lose these marks: never confuse regulate (maintains constant internal conditions) with conform (changes with environment), or hibernation (cold-avoidance) with aestivation (heat/desiccation-avoidance) – these are the most commonly swapped pairs in this chapter.
