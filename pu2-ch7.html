Board Important — Repeated Question Bank
All 25 questions with model answers, exactly as examiners expect them.

⭐⭐⭐ Top priority – almost always asked (5 marks)

  • Life cycle of Plasmodium (5 M) – two hosts (human + female Anopheles); sporozoites injected via saliva → hepatic schizogony in liver hepatocytes releasing merozoites → erythrocytic schizogony in RBCs releasing hemozoin (fever & chills) → gametocytogenesis (male & female gametocytes) → mosquito ingests gametocytes → syngamy forms ookinete → oocyst → sporogony produces sporozoites stored in salivary glands.
  • Structure of an antibody molecule (5 M) – draw the Y-shaped H2L2 monomer: 2 heavy + 2 light chains held by disulfide (–S–S–) bonds; label the variable region (VH, VL) at the amino-terminal tips as the antigen-binding site, and the constant region (CH, CL) at the carboxy-terminal stem.
  • Pathogenesis & replication of HIV/AIDS (5 M) – gp120 spike binds CD4 on macrophages/helper T-cells → reverse transcriptase makes viral DNA → integrase inserts it into host chromosome (macrophage becomes an "HIV factory") → virus enters and lyses helper T-cells → TH count falls below ~200 cells/μL → opportunistic infections (Mycoplasma, Toxoplasma, fungi) prove fatal. Diagnosis: ELISA (screening) → Western blotting (confirmation).
  • Essay on abused drugs (5 M) – opioids (Papaver somniferum → diacetylmorphine/heroin, binds opioid receptors, depressant), cannabinoids (Cannabis sativa, binds cannabinoid receptors in brain, affects cardiovascular system), coca alkaloids (Erythroxylum coca, blocks dopamine reuptake, CNS stimulant).

⭐⭐ High priority – frequently asked (3 marks)

  • Innate vs Acquired immunity (3 M) – innate: congenital, non-specific, no memory, 4 kinds of barriers; acquired: develops after pathogen contact, highly specific, has lifelong memory, mediated by B- and T-lymphocytes.
  • Active vs Passive immunity (3 M) – active: host's own B-cells make antibodies, slow onset, long-lasting (e.g. recovery from measles; vaccines like Polio/BCG/MMR); passive: ready-made antibodies given directly, immediate but short-lived (e.g. maternal IgG/colostrum IgA; anti-tetanus serum, anti-venom).
  • Four barriers of innate immunity (3 M) – physical (skin, mucus linings), physiological (HCl, lysozyme in tears/saliva, sebum), cellular (PMNL, monocytes, macrophages, NK cells), cytokine (interferons from virus-infected cells).
  • Primary vs secondary lymphoid organs (3 M) – primary: bone marrow & thymus, where lymphocytes mature; secondary: spleen, lymph nodes, tonsils, Peyer's patches, MALT, where mature lymphocytes meet antigens and mount a response.
  • Withdrawal syndrome & dependence (3 M) – dependence is the body's physiological/psychological adaptation to regular drug/alcohol use; withdrawal syndrome is the distressing symptoms (anxiety, tremors, sweating, nausea, vomiting) on abrupt discontinuation, relieved only by resuming use or clinical detox.

⭐ Two-mark favourites – learn as clean pairs

  • Benign tumours (confined, encapsulated) vs malignant tumours (invasive, metastasise to distant organs).
  • Allergens (pollen, dust – trigger IgE) · histamine & serotonin released by mast cells during allergic reactions.
  • Amoebiasis – pathogen Entamoeba histolytica, site large intestine, mechanical vector housefly.
  • Contact inhibition (normal cells stop dividing on contact) – lost in cancer cells, which divide continuously.
Answer-writing tip: for every 5-mark life-cycle or replication question, write it as a numbered flowchart (i)–(v/vii), not a paragraph – examiners award marks per correctly sequenced step.
Never lose these marks: always name the exact confirmatory test or molecule – Widal test (typhoid), hemozoin (malaria chills), IgA (colostrum), IgE (allergy), ELISA & Western blotting (HIV), H2L2 (antibody formula).

Immune System Diagrams

Fig 7.1 — Structure of an Antibody Molecule (H2L2) 3 – 5 M ★★★ Top Priority
Draw the Y-shaped symmetry with 4 chains – 2 long Heavy (H) + 2 short Light (L), formula H2L2. Label the inter-chain disulfide (–S–S–) bonds, the variable region (VH, VL) at the amino (N-terminal) tips as the antigen-binding site, and the constant region (CH, CL) at the carboxy (C-terminal) stems.
Fig 7.2 — Diagrammatic Representation of Lymphoid Organs 3 M ★★☆ High Priority
Show the human silhouette with: Thymus (bilobed, near the heart, shrinks with age), Bone marrow (inside long bones, produces all blood cells), Spleen (bean-shaped, left upper abdomen, filters blood), Lymph nodes (along lymphatic vessels), and MALT (Peyer's patches, appendix – 50% of lymphoid tissue).

Life Cycles & Viral Replication

Fig 7.3 — Schematic Life Cycle of Plasmodium vivax 5 M ★★★ Top Priority
Split the diagram into two hosts. Human side: sporozoites injected → liver hepatocytes (hepatic schizogony) → merozoites → RBCs (erythrocytic schizogony, hemozoin released) → male/female gametocytes. Mosquito side: gametocytes ingested → fertilisation forms ookinete → oocyst on gut wall → sporogony → sporozoites migrate to salivary glands.
Fig 7.4 — Replication of Retrovirus (HIV) Inside a Host Cell 3 – 5 M ★★☆ High Priority
Label in sequence: gp120 spikes bind CD4 receptors on a macrophage/helper T-cell → viral RNA uncoats → reverse transcriptase makes viral DNA → integrase inserts it into the host chromosome → host transcribes new viral RNA/proteins → new virions assemble and bud off to infect more T-cells. Note the macrophage surviving as a continuous "HIV factory".

Chemical Structures & Plant Sources

Fig 7.5 & 7.6 — Morphine & Cannabinoid Chemical Structures 2 M ★☆☆ Occasional
Morphine: phenanthrene-core skeleton, extracted from opium poppy latex; heroin is its acetylated (diacetylmorphine) form. Cannabinoid: resorcinol-terpene core with a pentyl side chain, from Cannabis sativa resin/flower tops. Label both as binding their respective receptors (opioid receptors in CNS/GI tract; cannabinoid receptors in the brain).
Fig 7.7 — Botanical Sources: Opium Poppy, Cannabis, Datura 2 – 3 M ★☆☆ Identification-based
Opium poppy (Papaver somniferum): unripe globular capsule with pink flowers, lanced to exude milky morphine-rich latex. Cannabis sativa: distinct serrated palmate leaflets; resin & flower tops give marijuana/hashish/charas/ganja. Datura: funnel-shaped solitary flowers; leaves/seeds contain hallucinogenic tropane alkaloids.
